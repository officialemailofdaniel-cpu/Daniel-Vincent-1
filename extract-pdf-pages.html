Extracting selected pages into a separate PDF

Page extraction creates a new PDF containing only selected pages from a larger document. It is useful for sharing a chapter, isolating an invoice or collecting supporting evidence without sending the complete source.

Plan the selection

List the pages that belong in the new file. Selected pages may be consecutive or spread across the document depending on the available controls. Use viewer page positions rather than printed labels.

Extraction versus splitting

Extraction is best when you need a selection from a larger PDF. Splitting is more suitable when you want to divide the whole document into multiple parts. Choose the workflow that matches the intended output.

Preserve reading order

For non-consecutive pages, consider how a recipient will understand the selection. Include context pages where necessary and use a descriptive filename that explains the contents.

Check the extracted result

Open the new PDF and confirm every intended page is present and ordered correctly. Inspect tables, diagrams, footnotes and attachments that may continue across page boundaries.

Remember that the original remains complete

Extraction creates a separate copy; it does not remove those pages from the source. Share only the intended output and follow data-handling rules for sensitive records.
