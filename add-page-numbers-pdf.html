Adding page numbers to a PDF document

Page numbers help readers navigate long documents, refer to specific sections and keep printed pages in order. Before adding numbering, decide where it should appear and whether the document already has a numbering convention.

Choose a numbering plan

Decide whether numbering begins on the first page, starts after a cover or uses a specific starting number. Formal submissions may have precise rules for placement and sequence.

Select a readable position

Numbers are often placed in a header or footer and aligned left, centre or right. Keep them away from body text, tables, footnotes and existing footer content. Choose a size that remains readable when printed.

Viewer positions and printed labels

The first PDF page may have a different printed page number. Front matter, Roman numerals and section-specific numbering create this difference. Check the source before choosing a starting value.

Check different page layouts

Portrait pages, landscape charts and unusual margins can affect placement. Inspect the first, middle and final pages, especially pages with narrow margins or dense tables.

Keep an unnumbered source

Preserve the original so you can create another version with a different starting point or style. Give the numbered copy a distinct filename.
