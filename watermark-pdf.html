Using PDF watermarks for identification and review

A watermark adds a visible label or graphic to PDF pages. It can identify a draft, indicate review status or show that a copy is intended for a particular purpose. It should communicate clearly without making the document difficult to read.

Choose accurate wording

Decide what the watermark must communicate. Labels such as Draft, Confidential and Sample have different meanings. Use accurate wording and mark documents only when you have authority.

Balance visibility and readability

A watermark that is too dark can obscure paragraphs, tables and signatures. One that is too faint may be missed. Consider placement, size, rotation and transparency, then inspect pages with dense content.

Select pages deliberately

Some documents have cover pages or signature pages that need different treatment. Decide whether the mark belongs on every page or only selected pages, and check both portrait and landscape layouts.

Watermarks are not access control

A visible label does not prevent copying, screenshots or editing. Do not rely on it to protect sensitive information. Use appropriate permissions and secure document-handling practices.

Review the marked copy

Inspect the watermark over text, photographs and charts. Confirm the wording is correct, no pages were missed and the document remains readable when printed. Keep an unmarked source copy.
